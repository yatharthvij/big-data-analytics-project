Model-evaluation caveat

0.998–0.999 weighted F1 is unrealistically high for a live fraud operation. This is a Kaggle synthetic dataset whose label generator likely composes abuse_type from a fairly clean rule — producing near-separable classes real transactional data never gives you. Presented as proof of the pipeline and methodology, not a performance guarantee. Realistic production target: 80–90% weighted F1. Full discussion in fraud-classification-results.md.

06Key findings, risks & recommendations

What the three models, taken together, tell us — and what to do about it.

Key findings

  • Non-legit returns skew expensive, not just frequent. 29.9% of orders carry 43.8% of refund $ ($4.64M) — Fraudulent Return claims average 3.1× a legitimate return.
  • The classifier earns its complexity. Random Forest hits perfect precision/recall on the binary framing where a free 4-flag heuristic tops out at F1 0.61.
  • The quality problem is broad-based, not one vendor. Quality-issue rates band 31.5%–35.2% across all 12 categories.
  • A simpler model wins honestly on refund pricing. Linear Regression beats both a naive baseline (40% RMSE) and a more complex GBTRegressor.
  • The dataset's fraud rate is industry-realistic. 17.9% sits close to Appriss Retail's reported 15.14%.

Risks & roadmap

  • RiskSynthetic-data overfit. Re-train on real labeled outcomes before go-live; budget for 80–90% F1, not 99.9%.
  • RiskNo production ground-truth labels. Build human-in-the-loop labeling from the review queue this system creates.
  • RiskQuality finding explains what, not why. Broad-based isn't root-caused — needs a packaging/supplier QA audit.
  • Roadmap. Real-time scoring at return-request time, a feedback loop from reviewer overrides, and a rolling time-based validation window once live data exists.
Ship this first — no ML required

Require photo evidence above a value threshold. The "high-value item + no photo evidence" pattern alone touches $1.88M of non-legitimate refund exposure across 4,673 orders — live immediately, independent of the classifier's rollout timeline.
